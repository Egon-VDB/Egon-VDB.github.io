[*Fragile Masculinity*][FM] is a game I developed during the [*UNWRAP gamejam of 2023*][UNWRAP] at the beginning of my internship at [MoonMonster Studios][MS], allowing me to immediately collaborate with my new colleagues.

Working closely as a team, we combined our ideas and skills to create a fun and engaging game, which ultimately went on to win the game jam.

## The game
The game is a satirical take on toxic masculinity and male fragility, where you play a girl trying to make her way home through hostile environments. Each area is guarded by a boss who represents a stereotypical bad male, bombarding the player with catcalls and insults in the form of a bullet hell.

The player fights back using witty comebacks and verbal counters, gradually breaking down the boss’s fragile ego until they break down and allow the player to progress. The gameplay turns dialogue into combat where you shoot out your remarks at your foes.

## Interpreting the theme
The game jam, hosted by [Digital Arts & Entertainment][DAE], revolved around the theme of “fragility.” This single word served as the only guideline.

We thought outside of the "fragile box, so to speak. Fragility, while definitely fitting to describe objects that break easily, can also take a more figurative meaning. Determined on winning, we worked to find an idea that would stand out, something with a more comedic edge. Then it hit us, *fragile masculinity*.

It had strong potential for a comedic tone while engaging the attention of the other participants. Our concept allowed for gameplay both fun and memorable. The player takes control of a girl walking home, but along her journey she encounters fragile men bombarding her with catcalls and unwanted "compliments". Armed with snide remarks, witty comebacks and not-so-kind words you dismantle their fragile maculinities, fighting your way home.

This wasn't my first project, but it certainly was one of the shortest ones. The game jam lasted only a week, and within that time we had to deliver a fully playable game. The clock was ticking from day one, yet the coordination within the team was incredible. Every day the game progressed further and further, soon turning from a fun prototype to a neatly polished tech demo.

By the end we had one of the best games in the jam, and we knew it. We eagerly put together an Itch.io page with all our accomplishments and made a trailer for the game.

## The winners
On the final day of the jam, all teams gathered to present their games. Each project was introduced with a trailer, followed by a short explanation from its creators, showcasing a wide variety of creative and interesting ideas. As the winners were announced, excitement filled the room third and second place teams stepped forward to accept their awards, clearly thrilled.

Then came the announcement of first place. The anticipation was palpable as everyone waited to hear which team had won. Finally, the moment arrived: our game was called. With our unique concept and engaging gameplay, we had won the competition. We stepped onto the stage, proud and excited, and gladly accepted our prize.

This experience set the tone for my internship and left me excited for what lay ahead

## Learning new things
The game jam set the tone for the rest of my internship. It showed me how much I would learn while working with MoonMonster. During my time at DAE, I had primarily focused on concept art and 3D game graphics, using Unreal Engine’s lighting tools to create sharp, high-resolution renders of realistic models.

This time, however, I was working on a 2D game, which pushed me to learn how to effectively use lighting systems alongside sprites. I transformed a flat, hand-drawn and painted environment into a vibrant night scene, bringing it to life with lighting from neon signs and glowing building windows.
