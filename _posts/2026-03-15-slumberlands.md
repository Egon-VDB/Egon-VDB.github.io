[*Slumberland*][SLUMBER] was the final group project I developed at DAE, serving as the primary focus of my fifth semester, which allowed me to fully dedicate myself to making it the best project it could be.

The game follows Mina, a young heroine who battles nightmare creatures to restore peace to Slumberland. Players explore two distinct islands: a factory filled with mechanical puzzles and a village consumed by darkness, each culminating in a unique boss encounter.

Several projects were pitched by third parties and students, with each student having to choose three. [My Machine][MACHINE] was one that stood out, because it involved bringing the creativity of children to life within a video game. Three other artists, two programmers and an Independent Game Production were assigned to the project with me, which allowed me to work with some very talented people.

The Independent Game Production student was a new addition, seeking to bring their unique skills into the group projects to elevate the quality. I used this opportunity to learn from curriculums outside my own. Although I had chosen Game Graphics Production as my major, I never wanted that to limit what I could learn from others.

## The core design challenge
<img src="https://usercontent.one/wp/www.mymachinevlaanderen.be/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/drawings-kids-mymachine-2048x977.png?media=1731595506" 
style="margin-left: 12.5%; width: 75%"/>

“How do you turn unstructured imagination into a playable game?” That was one of the biggest questions we faced at the start of development. With 43 drawings ranging from candy islands to terrifying clowns, we had a huge amount of creative but chaotic material to work with.

After plenty of brainstorming, we decided to focus on creating a platformer with light puzzle elements, a genre we felt was well suited for children. Every drawing found its way into the game in some form. Some became collectibles, while others inspired environmental props, characters, enemies, and interactive objects. To tie these wildly different ideas together, we embraced the dreamlike nature of the project and set the game within a surreal dream world.

One of our biggest challenges was finding the right balance between accessibility and challenge. Designing puzzles that were engaging without becoming frustrating for younger players proved more difficult than expected. To help with this, we regularly invited children to playtest the game, allowing us to refine mechanics and ensure the experience remained approachable for our target audience.

I primarily focused on the game’s shaders and character work, with shaders being a particularly new area for me at the time. In addition to the technical work, I also contributed much of the concept art for the village environment as well as two enemy designs.

In total, I created four character models. The first was Mina, the game’s protagonist, followed by the shadowy nightmare enemies, the flower robot, and the game’s large robotic boss. 

The boss proved especially challenging compared to the other models. While the humanoid characters could be rigged and animated using Mixamo, the robot required a completely custom rig due to its non-human proportions. I rigged and animated the entire character myself, marking my first real experience with custom 3D animation work.

On the technical side, I experimented heavily with shaders using Unity Shader Graph. By following tutorials and adjusting them to fit the project’s visual style, I implemented stylised water, animated grass, and cel shading effects. The cel shading system eventually became too complex for Shader Graph alone, however, and with production deadlines approaching, I decided to pivot to a third-party shader solution to keep development on schedule.

These contributions, combined with the environments created by my teammates and the strong technical work delivered by the programmers, helped bring together a vibrant and playful game that the children absolutely adored.

Developing Slumberland was both challenging and incredibly rewarding. The project pushed me far outside my comfort zone, from experimenting with shaders to creating and animating a fully custom rigged character for the first time.

More importantly, it taught me how to collaborate across disciplines and transform broad creative ideas into a cohesive experience. Seeing children enjoy the final game made all the challenges throughout development feel worthwhile.
