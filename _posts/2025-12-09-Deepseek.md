This is one of the most interesting tech & meets, hosted by Howest on Tuesdays, that I attended. It was an expansive talk of quite some length, but captivating throughout it.

With how common the use of AI has become, this is a talk of interest to everyone in my opinion. The talk explored the history of AI, the existing models and the AI race between America and China. The talk was given by Dimitri Casier, a teacher at Howest who has worked and read a lot about AIs, LLMs and prompt engineering.

The talk wasn't quite technical even though AI is complicated, with plenty of algorithms working together. Instead the focus was more about the history of AI, its uses and performances. The primary focus however was deepseek, how it came to be so big and its performance compared to GPT. I have some experience working with AI, having followed the Applied AI course, yet of the history I wasn't quite as knowledgeable. I was surprised to find out just how good deepseek is and plan to use it as opposed to solely relying on GPT when I occasionaly use AI.

The talk itself was great, with a nicely structured narrative revolving around Moby Dick and the whale. This gave the entire talk a nice flow and it fit the various topics quite well. I also learned a lot more about all the different tools available and while I am not planning to use most, knowing about them is quite handy in case the need arises.

I'd recommend this tech & meet to anyone with a passing interest in AI, whether it be the pratical application or the history. I myself would gladly attend it again as the history of AI is pakced with a lot of interesting facts.
