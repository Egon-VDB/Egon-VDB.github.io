[*Cloudy with a chance of Cannonballs*][ITCH] is the first group project I was a part of during my third semester at [Digital Arts & Entertainment][DAE]. Together with team of two oter artists, two programmers and a single sound designer, we created a game I am still immensely proud of today.

<!--more-->

In the game, players pilot their own airships through dangerous skies, earning points by blasting opponents and avoiding hazards. Matches are chaotic and fast-paced, with special ammunition types regularly appearing to shake up combat. Random events such as violent storms and rising bomb barrages further intensify the action, forcing players to constantly adapt in order to survive and claim the title of king of the skies.

Every year DAE selects one student project as the winner for an official Steam release. While we narrowly missed out, we were incredibly proud to have come so close.

The game is still available on [Itch.io][ITCH], making it easy to jump into a match with a friend using nothing more than two controllers and a competitive spirit.

## Finding the concept
<img src="../../../../img/Oq4uO.gif" 
style="margin-left: 12.5%; width: 75%;;"/>

When we started the project we didn't quite have an idea. It was up to us to come up with a unique game concept. We explored several ideas like a chasing game and a ghost game where you haunted fellow player. In the end, however, we went with our airship idea which proved to be a fun and engaging PvP game that allowed for creative visuals and gameplay.

The next step was creating a prototype. We weighed the pros and cons of Unity and Unreal, considering rendering pipelines, lighting methods, and programming conventions in relation to our needs. In the end, Unity came out on top.

During the first week of development, we focused heavily on concept art and defining the game’s visual identity. Together with Hanna, I helped create the art bible, a document that established the visual guidelines for everything from colour palettes to environmental styling. Having this foundation early on ensured that the game maintained a cohesive and recognisable art direction throughout production.

Once the visual language had been established, we divided responsibilities across the art team. Hanna focused on the airships, while Davide handled the environments and cloudscapes. I specialised in the game’s particle effects, creating the explosions, smoke, fire, and atmospheric effects that helped bring the chaotic aerial battles to life.

I was able to leverage my experience with animation to create custom spritesheets for smoke, fire and explosions, giving the game's special effects a far more energetic and stylised appearance. Using Unity's particle system, I integrated these effects throughout the game world, adding a stronger sense of movement, impact, and chaos to the aerial battles.

Alongside the particle work, I also contributed to the game’s lighting and overall atmosphere. One of the main challenges was balancing the warm, vibrant visual style with clear gameplay readability, ensuring players could always track hazards and opponents during fast-paced matches. Through extensive tweaking of the lighting and post-processing, we achieved a glowing colour palette that gave the game a distinctive visual identity.

That warm atmosphere contrasted especially well with one of the game’s random events: violent storms that transformed the skies from bright oranges into cold, stormy blues. The sudden shift in colour and mood added a dramatic sense of tension to matches, and to this day it remains one of my favourite visual elements in the project.
